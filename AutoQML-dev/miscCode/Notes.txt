######## Quantum autoencoder (Anomaly detection in high-energy physics using a quantum autoencoder)
- For normal, classical autoencoders, we train with data x and the output is x'. Our loss function compares x and x', and we aim to minimise their difference, ideally
x=x', even after passing through the latent space. We only train with background data, such that when we pass an anomaly (signal) through, x and x' are vastly different,
and we identify this as a signal.
- The classical loss function only considers reconstruction error, as their compression/latent space occurs naturally.
- In the quantum world, our loss function depends. If we keep the "trash wires", our loss should include a measure of them going to 0. If we SWAP out the trash wires, and 
replace them with fresh wires, then we don't need to include another term in the loss, as the trashing would then actually occur.
- Quantum autoencoders have the benefit that we only need to consider the encoding part, and train the trash state fidelity. As operations are unitary,
the decoder doesn't need to be trained, as if you can compress all info into the latent space, then the inverse unitary would uncompress it perfectly too. So the only
aim is to get a trash space fidelity of 1 with 0/reference/vacuum.
- So either we only train the encoder, and loss=trash fidelity. Or we form the full circuit, replace the trash wires and loss=reconstruction error.
- The autoencoders are trained to compress useful info, for background data. Any anomalies/signals may not have the same "useful info", and so the circuit Will
reconstruct them poorly.
- Simon thinks both would be interesting, but we can start with just the encoder training. Therefore, we just want to entangle info, then measure fidelity, no trashing needed.
